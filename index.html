Hocbigg - Journalism

🎓 Path to a free self-taught education in Journalism!

Last updated on: 2026-10-04

Introduction

Journalism is an empirical discipline of verification, inquiry, and public accountability. At its foundation, it is not merely the act of writing or broadcasting information, but a structured process of establishing verifiable facts, interrogating institutional power, and contextualizing current events for a self-governing public. Studying journalism equips learners with rigorous questioning techniques, evidentiary standards, and narrative precision—skills essential whether your goal is to report for a newsroom, run an independent publication, or critically navigate the contemporary information ecosystem.

This curriculum establishes the foundational core of an undergraduate journalism education. It is built for self-directed learners and requires no prior newsroom experience, media studies background, or institutional affiliation. Every subject included represents knowledge and practical craft that virtually every reporter, editor, and media practitioner must master before specializing.

Because journalism is an applied practice, this curriculum balances foundational conceptual texts with practical field guides and open courseware. It intentionally omits hyper-specialized subfields and academic communications research methods to keep your study focused on core newsgathering, writing, and editorial standards.

While individual subjects can be consulted independently, they are organized into a logical learning path from fundamental craft to complex reporting formats:

  • Foundational Craft and Reporting Routines: Begin with Foundations and History of Journalism to understand news values, press theories, and the democratic role of the Fourth Estate. Move immediately into News Writing and Reporting, Interviewing, Observation, and Sourcing, and Copy Editing and News Style. These subjects establish the primary mechanics of reporting: finding human sources, conducting interviews, structuring a hard-news story using the inverted pyramid, and editing copy for clarity, syntax, and accuracy.
  • Evidentiary and Legal Guardrails: Before reporting enterprise stories, work through Verification and Fact-Checking, Media Law and Freedom of Expression, and Media Ethics and Professional Standards. These subjects teach you how to authenticate user-generated content, navigate legal boundaries regarding defamation, privacy, and open records, and apply structured ethical decision-making to harm minimization and conflicts of interest.
  • In-Depth Investigation and Multiplatform Reporting: Once the basic craft and legal standards are established, proceed to Investigative Reporting and Public Records, Digital Storytelling and Social Media Reporting, Multimedia Journalism (Audio and Visual Storytelling), and Data Journalism and Numeracy. These subjects expand your toolkit into hypothesis-driven investigation, Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) requests, spreadsheet numeracy, and audio-visual packaging.

Mastering these foundational subjects prepares you to produce accurate, legally sound, and compelling news reporting. Once you have completed this core, you can branch out into practical and specialized extensions:

  • Explore specialized subfields—such as corporate financial forensics, cross-border collaborative reporting, or algorithmic accountability—in Advanced Topics.
  • Put these concepts into practice using structured reporting workflows, beat memos, and public records request templates in Projects.
  • Deepen your historical, sociological, and narrative perspective through landmark muckraking classics, press theory essays, and narrative nonfiction in Readings.
  • Supplement your reading with structured lecture series and video workshops led by investigative reporters and editors in Courses.

Curriculum

Foundations and History of Journalism

This subject covers the democratic purpose, constitutional role, historical evolution, and core operating principles of journalism as the Fourth Estate.

The Elements of Journalism: What Newspeople Should Know and the Public Should Expect (Bill Kovach & Tom Rosenstiel) - The primary theoretical text defining the democratic mandate, truth obligations, and core principles of the profession.

A History of News (Mitchell Stephens) - A historical companion to Kovach and Rosenstiel that traces how news gathering, dissemination, and public communication evolved from oral tradition to modern mass media.

What Is News? (Coursera / Michigan State University) - A structured video course introducing fundamental news values, audience trust, and reporting functions in modern media ecosystems.

News Writing and Reporting

This subject covers the core mechanics of newswriting, including the inverted pyramid structure, lead writing, beat reporting, and objective deadline drafting.

Inside Reporting: A Practical Guide to the Craft of Journalism (Tim Harrower) - The foundational visual textbook for mastering story architecture, hard-news leads, and beat coverage routines.

Become a Journalist: Report the News! Specialization (Coursera / Michigan State University) - An applied video sequence designed to be completed alongside Harrower, providing hands-on reporting assignments and structured peer reviews.

Interviewing, Observation, and Sourcing

This subject covers human source discovery, question design, interview psychology, field observation, and source management ethics.

Writing and Reporting News: A Coaching Method (Carole Rich) - The primary practical text using real-world newsroom examples to coach reporters on source development, note-taking, and listening techniques.

The Craft of Interviewing (John Brady) - A specialized companion to Rich that provides tactical guidance on handling reluctant subjects, off-the-record agreements, and conversational dynamics.

Gathering and Developing the News (Coursera / Michigan State University) - An interactive video course that provides structured exercises for identifying source networks and conducting preliminary interviews.

Copy Editing and News Style

This subject covers line editing, grammatical precision, Associated Press style guidelines, headline writing, and newsroom editorial standards.

The Art of Editing in the Age of Convergence (Brian S. Brooks & James L. Pinson) - The standard newsroom textbook teaching macro- and micro-editing, headline packaging, cutline formulation, and desk editorial judgment.

Good with Words: Writing and Editing Specialization (Coursera / University of Michigan) - A practical video companion to Brooks and Pinson focusing specifically on syntax flow, sentence-level concision, and systematic self-editing.

Verification and Fact-Checking

This subject covers digital content verification, image and video forensics, geolocation, source authentication, and open-source intelligence methods.

Verification Handbook: A Definitive Guide to Verifying Digital Content for Emergency Coverage (Craig Silverman, ed., European Journalism Centre) - The open-access field manual detailing professional workflows for authenticating user-generated media and real-time eyewitness claims.

Verification: The Basics (European Journalism Centre / DataJournalism.com) - A companion video course taught by the handbook's authors that demonstrates practical tools for geolocation, reverse image search, and digital provenance checks.

Crash Course: Navigating Digital Information (YouTube / CrashCourse & SHEG) - A fast-paced video series teaching lateral reading, click restraint, and evidentiary evaluation developed in partnership with the Stanford History Education Group.

Media Law and Freedom of Expression

This subject covers legal boundaries governing the press, including defamation and libel standards, invasion of privacy, reporter's privilege, copyright, and freedom of information laws.

Mass Media Law (Clay Calvert, Dan V. Kozlowski, & Derigan Silver) - The comprehensive academic textbook covering essential legal doctrines, First Amendment jurisprudence, and digital publishing liability.

The First Amendment Handbook (Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press) - A free, practical field manual that translates legal doctrine into immediate guidance on resisting subpoenas, protecting sources, and asserting public access rights.

Media Ethics and Professional Standards

This subject covers moral decision-making frameworks, conflict-of-interest management, harm minimization, anonymous sourcing, and newsroom accountability.

Doing Ethics in Media: Theories and Practical Applications (Jay Black & Chris Roberts) - The core textbook applying classical moral philosophies and a structured decision-making model to contemporary newsroom dilemmas.

Media Ethics & Governance (Coursera / University of Amsterdam) - An online lecture course that complements Black and Roberts by examining media accountability systems, institutional regulation, and moral reasoning.

Investigative Reporting and Public Records

This subject covers hypothesis-driven investigative inquiry, statutory public records retrieval (FOIA), document trails, court docket analysis, and source security.

Story-Based Inquiry: A Manual for Investigative Journalists (Mark Lee Hunter et al., UNESCO) - The foundational open-access manual that outlines how to organize long-term investigations through hypotheses, timelines, and structured evidence matrices.

The Investigative Reporter's Handbook: A Guide to Documents, Databases, and Techniques (Brant Houston & Investigative Reporters and Editors) - A detailed operational companion to Hunter that shows how to systematically locate, decode, and analyze public filings, court transcripts, and corporate databases.

Digital Storytelling and Social Media Reporting

This subject covers web-native journalism formats, live coverage, social newsgathering, mobile reporting workflows, and audience engagement strategies.

Journalism Next: A Practical Guide to Digital Innovation (Mark Briggs) - The core handbook covering the technological shifts, digital workflows, and publishing formats required in modern digital newsrooms.

Reuters Digital Journalism Course (Reuters & Meta Journalism Project) - A free, self-paced certification course designed to reinforce Briggs through interactive modules on mobile reporting, online newsgathering, and social curation.

Multimedia Journalism (Audio and Visual Storytelling)

This subject covers visual framing, news photojournalism, digital video production, field audio capture, podcast development, and multiplatform story packaging.

Multimedia Journalism: A Practical Guide (Andy Bull) - The comprehensive technical textbook detailing how to conceptualize, shoot, record, and assemble cross-platform visual and digital packages.

Sound Reporting: The NPR Guide to Audio Journalism and Production (Jonathan Kern) - A specialized companion to Bull focusing strictly on the audio medium, covering ambient sound recording, voice-track writing, and audio narrative structure.

Data Journalism and Numeracy

This subject covers newsroom math, spreadsheet analysis, dataset hygiene, rate calculations, and introductory data visualization for news stories.

The Data Journalism Handbook (Jonathan Gray, Liliana Bounegru, & Lucy Chambers, eds., European Journalism Centre) - An open-access guide detailing real-world data reporting case studies, source extraction methods, and newsroom workflows from international investigative teams.

Doing Journalism with Data: First Steps, Skills and Tools (European Journalism Centre / DataJournalism.com) - A structured video course providing hands-on instruction in spreadsheet formulas, pivot tables, data cleaning, and basic news charting tools.
